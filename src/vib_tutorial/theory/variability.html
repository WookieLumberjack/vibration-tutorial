No two structures are alike

Real masses, stiffnesses and joints scatter: from part to part, assembly to assembly, day to day. A model with nominal values predicts the average structure at best. Monte Carlo treats the properties as random: draw many structures, analyse each, and look at the spread of what matters. The page scatters every mass, spring and damper independently (lognormal, so they stay positive) and draws each copy's receptance and frequencies.

For small scatter the spread follows from the sensitivities (the Model updating page's): with φ mass-normalized, a spring's change moves λ = ω² by its strain energy share, (φi − φi−1)² δk, and a mass's by −λφi² δm, so to first order

σλ² = Σ ((φi − φi−1)² ki ck)² + Σ (λ φi² mi cm)²,   σf/f = σλ/2λ.

Frequencies scatter by less than the properties (each mode averages over many of them); peaks scatter sideways, so near a resonance the response's spread is huge. Damping's scatter moves only the peaks' heights. Design to the band, not the nominal curve.

Mode localization

A structure made of identical, weakly coupled parts (a bladed disc, a truss of identical bays, a row of identical rooms) has modes that are waves through all the parts, in a narrow band of frequencies. Make the parts slightly different (mistuning) and, when the differences are comparable to the coupling, the waves can no longer pass: each mode collapses onto one or a few parts (Anderson localization, first met in disordered crystals). The measure is the mistuning over the coupling, so a structure that is more precisely made but more weakly coupled can be worse.

The participation number (Σφ²)²/Σφ⁴ counts the parts a mode moves: two-thirds of N for a standing wave, N for the uniform one, 1 for a mode on one part.

Forced response of a mistuned disc

A turbine's blades are driven by the flow past the vanes ahead of them: the same force on every blade, its phase stepping 2πE/N from blade to blade (engine order E). A tuned disc answers with one travelling wave, every blade alike. Mistuned, the response spreads over several modes and concentrates on some blades: the worst blade's peak rises above the tuned one, which matters because fatigue is set by the worst blade. The amplification peaks when the mistuning is comparable to the coupling, and Whitehead's bound (1 + √N)/2 caps it for light damping. Intentional mistuning (alternating blades) is one of the cures.
