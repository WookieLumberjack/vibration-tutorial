Updating by sensitivities A measured FRF

No two structures are alike

Real masses, stiffnesses and joints scatter: from part to part, assembly to assembly, day to day. A model with nominal values predicts the average structure at best. Monte Carlo treats the properties as random: draw many structures, analyse each, and look at the spread of what matters. The page scatters every mass, spring and damper independently and draws each copy's receptance and frequencies.

Each property is its nominal value times a lognormal factor, which stays positive however wide the scatter. With the coefficient of variation V (the standard deviation over the mean: Vm, Vk, Vc for the masses, springs and dampers), the factor's logarithm is normal with

\sigma_L^2 = \ln\left(1 + V^2\right), \qquad \mu_L = -\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_L^2

the mean μL pulled down by half the variance so that the factor's own mean is 1. For a small V, σL ≈ V and the factor is nearly normal; for a wide one it leans: a few parts well above nominal, none below zero.

The frequencies' spread to first order

For a small scatter the spread follows from the sensitivities (the Model updating page's). With φr mass-normalized, a relative change in spring i moves ωr² by its share si of the mode's strain energy, and one in mass i by minus its share ti of the kinetic energy:

s_i = \frac{k_i\,(\phi_{ir} - \phi_{i-1,r})^2}{\omega_r^2}, \qquad t_i = m_i\,\phi_{ir}^2

each set adding up to 1 over the mode. Independent changes add in quadrature, and fr moves by half of ωr²'s relative change, so

\frac{\sigma_f}{f_r} = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{V_k^2 \sum_i s_i^2 + V_m^2 \sum_i t_i^2}

This is the dashed curve on the Frequencies tab and the first-order column of the readout. Since the shares add to 1, Σsi² is at most 1 (one spring carrying the whole mode) and at least one over the number of springs (all alike): a mode averages over the parts it moves, so its frequency scatters by less than half of theirs. Dampers don't enter at all: the undamped frequencies depend on M and K alone.

Why the peaks scatter most

The frequencies scatter by a per cent or two; the response near a resonance by far more, because the peak moves sideways. Near mode r one term of the receptance dominates. If the scattered mode sits a fraction δ above the nominal one, then at the nominal resonance ωr²(1 + δ)² − ωr² ≈ 2δωr² beside the damping's 2ζωr², and

\frac{|H(\omega_r)|}{|H|_{\text{peak}}} \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + (\delta/\zeta)^2}}

The yardstick is the frequencies' scatter against the damping ratio: once σf/fr is comparable to ζ, the response at the nominal peak can drop to half or less, and between the peaks a scattered peak can arrive and raise it. The band on the receptance is therefore narrow between the peaks and wide at them. Damping's own scatter changes only the peaks' heights. Design to the band, not the nominal curve.

How many draws

Monte Carlo's answer is itself random. A standard deviation estimated from Ns normal draws scatters by about

\frac{\sigma(\hat\sigma)}{\sigma} \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,(N_s - 1)}}

so 200 chains give each σf to about 5 %, and halving that takes four times as many. The band's edges (the 5th and 95th percentiles) rest on the few chains out in the tails and need more draws than the standard deviation does.

Mode localization

A structure made of identical, weakly coupled parts (a bladed disc, a truss of identical bays, a row of identical rooms) has modes that are waves through all the parts, in a narrow band of frequencies. The page's model is a ring of N blades, each a mass m on a spring k to a disc held still, neighbours joined by springs kc:

Tuned, a wave with n nodal diameters, xj = Xei2πnj/N, stretches each coupling spring by X(ei2πn/N − 1), and the blade's equation gives

\omega^2 = \frac{k}{m}\left(1 + \frac{4k_c}{k}\sin^2\frac{\pi n}{N}\right), \qquad n = 0, 1, \ldots, N/2

from the blade's own frequency (n = 0, the coupling springs unstretched) to √(1 + 4kc/k) times it (neighbours in opposition). The waves round the ring one way and the other share a frequency, so every mode but the first and (for even N) the last comes in a pair.

Make the blades slightly different (mistuning: blade j's stiffness k(1 + δj)) and, when the differences are comparable to the coupling, the waves can no longer pass: each mode collapses onto one or a few blades. This is Anderson localization, first met in disordered crystals anderson and shown in structures hodges. Two blades show why. Their stiffness factors differ by Δ; the coupling kc is the off-diagonal term of their stiffness matrix and kΔ the difference on its diagonal, so the lower mode is (cos θ, sin θ) with

\tan 2\theta = \frac{2k_c}{k\,\Delta}

Tuned, θ = 45°: both blades alike. Once kΔ passes kc, θ falls and the mode settles on the softer blade:

The measure is the mistuning over the coupling, not the mistuning alone, so a structure that is more precisely made but more weakly coupled can be worse. The participation number

P = \frac{\left(\sum_j \phi_j^2\right)^2}{\sum_j \phi_j^4}

counts the blades a mode moves: N when they all move alike, about two-thirds of N for a standing wave (sin² averages ½ and sin⁴ ⅜), 1 for a mode on one blade, and 1/(cos⁴θ + sin⁴θ) for the pair.

Forced response of a mistuned disc

A turbine's blades are driven by the flow past the vanes ahead of them: the same force on every blade, its phase stepping round the disc,

F_j = F\,\mathrm{e}^{\,\mathrm{i}\,2\pi E j/N}

with E the engine order (the number of disturbances per revolution). A tuned disc answers with the matching wave alone, every blade alike: n = E, or N − E (a wave the other way) when E passes N/2, as the samples of a sine alias. Its peak, at that wave's frequency, is set by the damper alone. Mistuned, the force's wave is no longer a mode: the response spreads over several modes and concentrates on some blades, and the worst blade's peak rises above the tuned one. That matters because fatigue is set by the worst blade. The amplification peaks when the mistuning is comparable to the coupling, and for light damping and one engine order it can't exceed Whitehead's bound whitehead

\frac{|x|_{\max}}{|x|_{\text{tuned}}} \le \frac{1 + \sqrt{N}}{2}

With much more mistuning the blades decouple and each answers alone: the amplification falls back. Intentional mistuning (alternating two kinds of blade) is one of the cures: a large, known difference that keeps the random one small beside it castanier-pierre.

Worked example

The page's defaults: the uniform 4-mass chain (1 kg, 400 N/m, 2 N·s/m; modes at 1.105, 3.183, 4.877 and 5.982 Hz), its springs scattered by Vk = 5 %, 200 chains; and a ring of 12 blades (1 kg, 10 kN/m, kc = 5 % of k, ζ = 0.003), tuned.

  1. The factors. σL² = ln(1 + 0.05²) = 0.002497, so σL = 0.0500 and μL = −0.00125: a spring's 95th percentile is e−0.00125 + 1.645 × 0.05 = 1.084 times nominal.
  2. Mode 1's shares. Its springs carry 0.431, 0.333, 0.184 and 0.052 of the strain energy (twice the Model updating page's sensitivities). Σsi² = 0.333 (it is ⅓ for every mode of this chain), so σf/f1 = ½ × 5 % × √0.333 = 1.44 %: σf = 0.0160 Hz on 1.105 Hz, and 1.44 % for every mode.
  3. Against Monte Carlo. The 200 chains give 0.0155 Hz for mode 1 (1.40 %) and 1.36 to 1.45 % over the modes. With 200 draws the estimate is good to 1/√398 = 5.0 %, ± 0.0008 Hz: the two agree.
  4. At the peak. Mode 1's damping ratio is 0.0174, a little more than the 1.44 % scatter. 95 % of the chains have their mode within 1.96 × 1.44 = 2.82 % of nominal, which leaves 1/√(1 + (2.82/1.74)²) = 0.53 of the peak; the readout's band at the nominal peak runs from 0.54 to 1.00 times nominal. It can't rise above 1: there the nominal chain is already at its peak.
  5. The tuned ring. The blade alone: √(10 000/1)/2π = 15.92 Hz. With kc/k = 0.05 the band runs to 15.92 × √1.2 = 17.43 Hz, through 16.02, 16.31, 16.69, 17.07 and 17.34 Hz for n = 1 to 5: the Ring modes tab's frequencies. Engine order 3 drives the n = 3 pair at 16.69 Hz, where the damper (2ζmωb at the blade's ωb) leaves 1/(2 × 0.003 × 100.0 × 104.9) = 0.0159 m/N on every blade.
  6. Two blades. Coupled by kc = 1 % of k, their stiffnesses 1 % apart: tan 2θ = 2, θ = 31.7°, amplitudes 0.85 and 0.53, P = 1.67. 5 % apart: θ = 10.9°, amplitudes 0.98 and 0.19, P = 1.07, a mode of one blade.
  7. The bound. For 12 blades Whitehead's (1 + √12)/2 = 2.23: the worst blade can answer at most 2.23 times the tuned ring's 0.0159 m/N, 0.0355 m/N, however the blades are mistuned.

Things to try

Each of these sets up the scatter or the ring (Things to try, top left); the first three load the uniform chain into the Simulation page.

  • Scattered properties scatter the frequencies by less, and the response near a peak by much more: compare σf/f with ζ.
  • First-order sensitivities predict the frequencies' spread from the strain and kinetic energy shares.
  • Weakly coupled, nearly identical parts localize their modes when the mistuning matches the coupling.
  • A mistuned disc's worst blade can answer up to (1 + √N)/2 times the tuned one.
