Forced response The impulse response and the FRF

A periodic force is a sum of harmonics

A force that repeats every T = 1/f₀, however it is shaped, is a Fourier series:

f(t) = a₀ + Σk≥1 (ak cos kω₀t + bk sin kω₀t),   ω₀ = 2πf₀,

a₀ its mean, ak = (2/T)∫f cos kω₀t dt and bk = (2/T)∫f sin kω₀t dt. Only multiples of f₀ appear: a periodic force has a line spectrum. Written with complex amplitudes, f = Re Σ Ck eikω₀t, Ck = ak − i bk.

Each harmonic through H

The oscillator is linear, so each harmonic is a harmonic force on its own (the Single oscillator page's forced response), and the steady response is the sum of their responses:

x(t) = Re Σ H(kω₀) Ck eikω₀t,   H(ω) = 1/(k − ω²m + iωc).

The mean only sags the spring (H(0) = 1/k). A harmonic that lands near fn is amplified by Q; the rest are not. So a force at f₀ can resonate the oscillator at any multiple k f₀ = fn it contains: a machine running at a third of a mode's frequency can still drive it, through its third harmonic.

Shapes and their harmonics

  • Square wave (±F): (4F/π) Σ sin kω₀t / k over odd k. A wave with the half-wave symmetry f(t + T/2) = −f(t) has odd harmonics only.
  • Sawtooth: (2F/π) Σ (−1)k+1 sin kω₀t / k, every harmonic.
  • Triangle: (8F/π²) Σ ± sin kω₀t / k², odd only.
  • Rectified sine F|sin πt/T|: 2F/π − (4F/π) Σ cos kω₀t / (4k² − 1).
  • Rectangular pulses (F for a fraction d of each period): mean Fd; |Ck| = (2F/πk)|sin πkd|, zero at every multiple of 1/d.
  • Impulse train (a blow I every T): I/T + (2I/T) Σ cos kω₀t: every harmonic the same height.

How fast the harmonics fall depends on how smooth the wave is: as 1/k with a jump, 1/k² with a kink (a jump in slope), and not at all for an impulse. A jump also brings the Gibbs phenomenon: the series overshoots it by 9 % of the jump however many terms it keeps, the overshoot only getting narrower.

The steady state without the series

The page also works the steady state out directly. One period from rest gives zT = ΦTz₀ + zp (ΦT = eAT, the force stepped exactly by the first-order hold); a periodic state has zT = z₀, so z₀ = (I − ΦT)−1zp. That is exact, and it is what the series converges to. Without damping, a harmonic exactly on fn leaves I − ΦT singular: no steady state, the swing grows forever.

Starting from rest

Switched on from rest, the response is the steady state plus a free vibration that makes up the start, as for a single sine; it dies away in a few time constants 1/ζωn. The From rest tab steps it.

Things to try

Each of these sets up the oscillator, the force and the plots (Things to try, top left).

  • A periodic force is a line spectrum: its mean and harmonics at whole multiples of f₀.
  • Each harmonic goes through H(kω₀) on its own; a mode at any k f₀ resonates.
  • The harmonics fall as 1/k after a jump, 1/k² after a kink; a jump's series overshoots it by 9 % (Gibbs).
  • The steady state can be found exactly from one period: z₀ = (I − ΦT)−1zp.
