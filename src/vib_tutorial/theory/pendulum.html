Turning instead of sliding

A body turning about a fixed axis is the single oscillator with every quantity rotated:

slidingturning
x (m)θ (rad)
m (kg)J (kg·m²), the moment of inertia about the axis
k (N/m)kθ (N·m/rad)
c (N·s/m)cθ (N·m·s/rad)
F (N)M (N·m)

so J θ'' + cθ θ' + kθ θ = M, ωn = √(kθ/J), ζ = cθ/2√(kθJ), and every formula of the Single oscillator page holds with these in place of m, k and c. The moment of inertia about a pivot a distance d from the centre of mass is J = JG + m d² (parallel axes).

Gravity as a spring

A pendulum has no spring: its weight, acting a distance d from the pivot, gives a restoring moment m g d sin θ. For small swings sin θ ≈ θ, so kθ = m g d and

ωn = √(m g d / J);   a simple pendulum (J = mL², d = L): ωn = √(g/L).

The mass cancels for a simple pendulum, so its period depends only on L and g. A body of any shape swings as a simple pendulum of length Leq = J / m d. For a uniform rod J = m(L²/12 + d²), so the period is least when d equals the radius of gyration L/√12, and each longer period is reached at two pivots, d and kG²/d (Kater's reversible pendulum: the distance between those two pivots is Leq).

Turned upside down, the weight pushes the body over: the stiffness is kθ − m g d, and if the spring is weaker than m g d it is negative, upright is unstable, and the body falls to a leaning balance, as a column buckles. Gravity (and any steady load) changes stiffness: this is the geometric stiffness a tension or compression adds to a string, a cable or a column.

Large swings

sin θ grows more slowly than θ, so a hanging pendulum's spring softens as its swing grows, and its period lengthens. Energy gives it exactly: the swing to ±A takes

T = 4 ∫0A dθ / √(2(V(A) − V(θ))/J),

for the simple pendulum T = T₀ (2/π) K(sin²(A/2)), with K the complete elliptic integral: 1.8 % longer at 30°, 18 % at 90°, without bound as A → 180°. Keeping one more term, sin θ ≈ θ − θ³/6, makes it a Duffing oscillator with γ = −mgd/6, and its first harmonic balance gives ω² ≈ ω₀²(1 + 3γA²/4k), T ≈ T₀(1 + A²/16): good for moderate swings. The inverted pendulum's cubic has the other sign, so it hardens. A torsion wire is linear, so a disc on one keeps its period at any twist.

In the phase plane (θ, θ') the undamped motion follows the curves of constant energy. Small orbits are ellipses; larger ones flatten at the ends; the separatrix through the upside-down balance divides swinging from going round and round, and an orbit near it lingers at the top.

Things to try

Each of these sets up the pendulum and the plots (Things to try, top left).

  • Turning is sliding with J for m, kθ for k and moments for forces.
  • A weight a distance d below the pivot is a spring kθ = m g d; above it, a negative one.
  • A simple pendulum's period, 2π√(L/g), doesn't depend on its mass; a rod's is least pivoted at its radius of gyration.
  • Large swings soften a hanging pendulum (a Duffing oscillator with a negative cubic) and harden an inverted one.
