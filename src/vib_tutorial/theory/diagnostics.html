What a machine's vibration says

Every part of a running machine leaves its own frequencies in the casing's vibration: the shafts their speeds and harmonics (unbalance at 1X, misalignment at 2X), the gears their tooth-mesh frequency (teeth × the shaft's speed), the rolling bearings their defect frequencies. Condition monitoring reads the spectrum against that list. A fault changes a line, adds new ones, or adds a pattern: sidebands, impacts, families of harmonics. This page synthesizes a gearbox's casing acceleration and looks at it four ways.

Run-ups and order tracking

While the speed changes, a frequency analysis smears: a fixed-length block sees the 1X sweep across several lines. Each force, though, stays at a fixed order (a multiple of the speed), so order tracking analyses in angle instead of time: the record is resampled at equal steps of the shaft's angle (from a tachometer's pulses) and each block of whole revolutions is transformed. An order is then one line, whatever the speed did. Against frequency, the orders are rays from the origin and a structural mode a horizontal band they light up as they cross; against order the orders stand still and the mode's band slants. Each order's amplitude along the run-up is its order track, the Bode plot of the Jeffcott rotor page measured.

A run-up that is too fast misreads a resonance: the response needs about Q cycles to build up, so a sweep that crosses the half-power band in fewer peaks lower and later, and rings after.

Gear-mesh sidebands

The mesh's force repeats at every tooth, at fm = z fshaft, with harmonics. A fault on one gear modulates it once a turn of that gear, which puts sidebands at fm ± k fshaft. How many tells what kind of fault: a smooth modulation (an eccentric or bent gear) gives one or two pairs; a short event once a turn (a cracked or broken tooth) gives a long, flat family, since a narrow pulse's spectrum is broad. Amplitude and phase modulation together make the pairs unequal. The spacing says which shaft the faulty gear is on.

Bearings and the envelope

A rolling bearing with a spall gives a strike each time a ball meets it. Where depends on the geometry (n balls of diameter d on a pitch circle D, contact angle α, the inner race turning at fr):

FTF = fr/2 (1 − d/D cos α),   BPFO = n FTF,   BPFI = n fr/2 (1 + d/D cos α),   BSF = D fr/2d (1 − (d/D cos α)²).

Each strike is a short impulse that rings the structure's resonances, in the kilohertz. The strikes' rate carries little energy at its own frequency, so the plain spectrum barely shows it under the shafts and the mesh. Envelope analysis band-passes the record round a resonance the strikes ring (clear of the gears), takes the envelope (the magnitude of the analytic signal, by the Hilbert transform), and transforms that: the strikes' rate, and its harmonics, stand out. The kurtosis of the band (3 for Gaussian noise) climbs with impacts and helps pick the band. Inner-race and ball defects pass through the load zone, so their strikes swell and fade: sidebands at ± 1X (inner race) or ± FTF (ball). The balls slip a little, so the strikes' spacing scatters by a percent or two: the envelope's lines stay sharp at low harmonics, while the raw spectrum's high ones smear.

The cepstrum

A family of lines spaced Δf is a periodic ripple in the log spectrum. The cepstrum, the inverse Fourier transform of the log power spectrum, turns that ripple into one peak at the quefrency 1/Δf (and its rahmonics). A gear's sidebands, however many and however unequal, become one peak at 1/fshaft; the mesh's own harmonics another at 1/fm. The log also separates a source from the path it passes through, which multiply in the spectrum and add in the cepstrum.

Things to try

Each of these sets up the machine (Things to try, top left).

  • Each part of a machine has its own frequencies; a diagnosis reads the spectrum against them.
  • Under a changing speed, track orders: resample at equal angles, and each order stays on one line.
  • Sidebands at the mesh ± the shaft's speed point to a gear; a long flat family to a local fault.
  • Bearing strikes hide in the plain spectrum; band-pass round a resonance and look at the envelope's.
  • The cepstrum turns a family of equally spaced lines into one peak at 1/spacing.
