Stepping an equation of motion

Most structures are solved in time by stepping Mẍ + Cẋ + Kx = f(t) with a fixed time step Δt, from xn at tn = nΔt to xn+1. A scheme replaces the derivatives with differences, and each choice gets the motion wrong in its own way. For a linear system the error is easy to see: let the scheme step a free, undamped oscillator of natural frequency ω and period T = 2π/ω. Its exact motion goes round a circle in the plane of (x, ẋ/ω), turning ωΔt a step. One step of any scheme is a fixed 2 × 2 (or 3 × 3) matrix, the amplification matrix, and its eigenvalues

\lambda = \rho\,\mathrm{e}^{\pm\mathrm{i}\theta}

say everything. The exact step has ρ = 1 and θ = ωΔt; a scheme's differ.

ρ > 1 (ρ is the spectral radius): the motion grows every step, and the scheme is unstable at this Δt.

ρ < 1: it shrinks, numerical damping. Per step the motion is multiplied by eln λ, as a damped oscillator's by e(−ζ ± i√(1−ζ²))ωΔt, so

\bar\zeta = \frac{-\ln\rho}{|\ln\lambda|} = \frac{-\ln\rho}{\sqrt{(\ln\rho)^2 + \theta^2}}

θ ≠ ωΔt: the computed period T̄ = 2πΔt/θ is wrong, a period elongation:

\frac{\bar T - T}{T} = \frac{\omega\Delta t}{\theta} - 1

All of these depend only on ωΔt = 2πΔt/T, so each scheme's behaviour is a set of curves against Δt/T (the tabs on the right).

Central difference

Central difference takes the acceleration from three positions:

\ddot x_n \approx \frac{x_{n+1} - 2x_n + x_{n-1}}{\Delta t^2}

It is explicit (no equations to solve when M is diagonal) and second order. On the undamped oscillator it gives xn+1 = (2 − ω²Δt²)xn − xn−1, so its eigenvalues solve λ2 − (2 − ω2Δt2)λ + 1 = 0. Their product is 1: while they are complex, both have ρ = 1 (no numerical damping) and

\theta = 2\arcsin\frac{\omega\Delta t}{2}

which is more than ωΔt, so the period comes out short. Once ωΔt > 2 there is no such angle: the roots are real, one outside the unit circle. So it is stable only for ωΔt ≤ 2, for the highest ω in the model: Δt ≤ Tmin/π. On a mesh this is the Courant condition cfl of explicit crash and blast codes: a fine mesh has a very fast highest mode, so the step has to be tiny however slow the motion of interest.

Newmark and HHT-α

Newmark's scheme newmark assumes how the acceleration varies over a step, with two parameters β and γ:

x_{n+1} = x_n + \Delta t\,\dot x_n + \Delta t^2\bigl[(\tfrac12 - \beta)\ddot x_n + \beta\,\ddot x_{n+1}\bigr] \dot x_{n+1} = \dot x_n + \Delta t\bigl[(1 - \gamma)\ddot x_n + \gamma\,\ddot x_{n+1}\bigr]

The equation of motion at n + 1 closes it: an implicit scheme, with a linear solve each step.

Average acceleration (γ = ½, β = ¼, the trapezoidal rule): the acceleration is the mean of its two ends. On the undamped oscillator

\lambda = \frac{1 - \omega^2\Delta t^2/4 \pm \mathrm{i}\,\omega\Delta t}{1 + \omega^2\Delta t^2/4}, \qquad \theta = 2\arctan\frac{\omega\Delta t}{2}

so ρ = 1 at every step: unconditionally stable, with no numerical damping, and second order. θ < ωΔt, so the period comes out long, by about (ωΔt)2/12.

  • Linear acceleration (γ = ½, β = 1/6): more accurate, but stable only for ωΔt ≤ 2√3.
  • γ > ½ damps, but drops to first order. HHT-α hht instead evaluates the stiffness and damping forces at tn+1+α, with γ = ½ − α, β = (1 − α)2/4 and α from −1/3 to 0. It is still second order and unconditionally stable, with damping that grows with ωΔt. It removes the unresolved high modes' noise and barely touches the low ones: the default in many finite-element codes.

Runge–Kutta

Classical RK4 (runge, kutta) on the first-order form ż = Az + Bf is fourth order. On a linear system its four stages add up to the Taylor series of the exact step eAΔt, cut after the fourth power:

z_{n+1} = \Bigl(I + A\Delta t + \frac{(A\Delta t)^2}{2} + \frac{(A\Delta t)^3}{6} + \frac{(A\Delta t)^4}{24}\Bigr) z_n

On the undamped oscillator AΔt has the eigenvalues ±iωΔt, and the missing terms make

\rho^2 = 1 - \frac{(\omega\Delta t)^6}{72} + \frac{(\omega\Delta t)^8}{576}

It is very accurate in period at small steps, and it damps: ρ < 1 up to its limit, ωΔt ≤ 2√2, where the last two terms cancel. It is explicit, so conditionally stable. Explicit Euler, zn+1 = zn + Δt żn, keeps only the first two terms: for an undamped oscillator λ = 1 ± iωΔt and ρ = √(1 + ω2Δt2) > 1 at every Δt. It steps along the tangent, which always points out of the circle, and the energy grows every step.

The exact update

For a linear model there is a better way. Over a step the free motion is exactly zn+1 = eAΔtzn. If the force is taken as straight between samples (a first-order hold), its part is exact too, two fixed matrices Γ0 and Γ1 worked out once:

z_{n+1} = \mathrm{e}^{A\Delta t} z_n + \Gamma_0 f_n + \Gamma_1 f_{n+1}

This FOH update is what the Simulation page and the One mass pages use. It is stable and exact in period and amplitude at any Δt, for any stiffness (ρ = e−ζωΔt, the oscillator's own). Its only error is in the force between samples, which is second order in Δt. It needs the matrix exponential, which is cheap for small models: big nonlinear codes, whose matrices change every step, still use Newmark or central difference.

Worked example

The page opens on 1 kg on 400 N/m, undamped, let go from 10 mm: ω = 20 rad/s (3.18 Hz), T = 0.314 s, and ten steps a period, Δt = 0.0314 s and ωΔt = 0.628. Each scheme's step:

  • Central difference: θ = 2 arcsin 0.314 = 0.639, so the period is 0.628/0.639 = 0.983 of the true one, 1.7 % short.
  • Average acceleration: θ = 2 arctan 0.314 = 0.609, the period 3.2 % long ((ωΔt)2/12 = 3.3 % estimates it).
  • Linear acceleration: 1.6 % long.
  • RK4: ρ = 0.99959 and 0.11 % long, with numerical damping ζ̄ = 0.065 %.

Over the ten periods, 100 steps, the errors pile up as phase. Average acceleration has turned 100 × 0.609 = 60.9 rad where the oscillator turned 62.8, 1.95 rad (0.31 of a cycle) behind, so it ends at 10 cos 1.95 = −3.7 mm where the mass is back at 10 mm. Central difference, 1.08 rad ahead, ends at 4.7 mm. RK4 is only 0.07 rad behind, but ρ100 = 0.960, so it ends at 9.6 mm. Against the 10 mm swing, the readout's largest errors are 98 % (central difference), 165 % (average acceleration), 91.9 % (linear acceleration) and 7.58 % (RK4): ten steps a period is enough for stability, not for ten periods of phase.

Things to try

Each of these sets up the oscillator, the step and the schemes (Things to try, top left).

  • One step of a scheme on a free oscillator is a matrix: its eigenvalues give stability (ρ ≤ 1), period error and numerical damping, all functions of ωΔt.
  • Explicit schemes are conditionally stable, and the limit is set by the fastest mode in the model: ωΔt ≤ 2 for central difference.
  • Newmark's average acceleration is stable at any step but lengthens the period; HHT-α adds damping that grows with frequency, removing unresolved modes.
  • Stable is not accurate: resolve the modes you care about with ten or more steps a period, and more for long runs.
  • For a linear model the FOH update is exact at any step, apart from the force between samples.
