Crossings and peaks

The S–N curve

A part loaded with constant-amplitude cycles of stress range S fails after N(S) cycles, and over the range that matters a straight line in log–log does: Basquin's law, N Sb = K. Welded steel details have b ≈ 3 (a detail category is the range they survive for two million cycles); smooth machined parts 5 to 10. A steep curve means the largest cycles do almost all the damage.

Miner's rule

Under cycles of many ranges, each cycle at S uses up 1/N(S) of the life, and failure is predicted when D = Σ ni/Ni reaches 1. It ignores the order of the cycles and is only good to a factor of two or so in practice, but it is what every code uses.

Rainflow counting

A random stress isn't made of cycles, so they have to be found. Rainflow counting (Matsuishi and Endo) pairs each reversal with the one that closes a hysteresis loop with it: the small ripples riding on a large swing become small cycles, and the large swing is still counted at its full range. The three-point form (ASTM E1049): keep a stack of reversals; when the latest range X is at least the one before, Y, then Y is a cycle (half a cycle if it holds the start) and its two points go. What is left at the end counts as half cycles.

Damage from the PSD

Without a record, everything has to come from the stress PSD G(f) through its moments mn = ∫ fⁿG df: the RMS σ = √m₀, the up-crossings ν₀ = √(m₂/m₀) and the peaks νp = √(m₄/m₂) per second (Rice, as on the Random vibration page), and the irregularity factor α₂ = ν₀/νp: 1 when every peak is followed by a zero crossing (a narrow band), smaller when ripples ride on the swings.

  • Narrow band (Bendat): each up-crossing is one cycle whose amplitude is Rayleigh in σ, so the range S = 2a and D/T = ν₀ (2√2 σ)b Γ(1 + b/2) / K. Exact for a narrow band; for a broad one it counts every ripple as a full swing and is conservative.
  • Dirlik's range distribution is an empirical fit to rainflow counts of many simulated processes: an exponential and two Rayleigh terms whose weights come from m₀, m₁, m₂ and m₄. D/T = νp E[Sb]/K. It is usually within 10 % of rainflow on the record, and the method most codes now recommend.

The record

The page synthesizes a Gaussian record with the PSD (each line with a random phase) and counts it. A long record is what the spectral methods stand for; a short one scatters from seed to seed, because a few large cycles carry most of the damage.

Things to try

Each of these sets up the stress, the curve and the record (Things to try, top left).

  • Basquin's S–N curve, N Sb = K, makes damage go as the stress to the power b: the RMS must be right.
  • Miner's rule adds n/N over the cycles; rainflow counting finds the cycles in a random record.
  • The narrow-band formula is exact for one narrow mode and conservative for broad or multi-modal stress.
  • The irregularity factor α₂ = ν₀/νp says how narrow the band is; Dirlik's formula handles any α₂.
