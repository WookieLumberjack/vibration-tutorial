Is it linear? Homogeneity

Everything a modal test fits assumes the structure is linear: double the force and every response doubles, so the FRF X/F is the same at any level. That's homogeneity, and the first check is to measure the FRF at two or three levels and lay them over each other. A linear structure's coincide; a nonlinear one's move with the level. Joints, contacts, friction and large deflections all do it, and only at the levels where they act: a gap that never closes is invisible. Test at the levels the structure works at.

Stepped sine, up and down

A stepped sine holds each frequency until the response is steady, reads its amplitude and phase, and steps on. Swept up and then down, a linear structure gives the same curve both ways. A hardening spring's peak leans to higher frequencies as the level rises, so the response has two steady states over a band: swept up it rides the upper branch until that ends and drops off; swept down it climbs onto it late. The two sweeps disagree between the jumps, which no linear structure can do. (The Duffing & Mathieu page, later, follows one nonlinear oscillator through its jump, chaos and all.)

Harmonic balance

The page finds each step's steady state by harmonic balance with one harmonic, x = Re(X eiωt), each nonlinear force replaced by its first harmonic, its describing function: (K − ω²M + iωC)X + N(X) = F. A cubic spring on a stretch of amplitude A adds a stiffness ¾k₃A²; a gap of clearance g, kg[1 − (2/π)(asin r + r√(1 − r²))] with r = g/A (nothing while A ≤ g); Coulomb friction Fc acts as a damper 4Fc/(πωA), weaker as the motion grows. Each step starts Newton from the last step's X, as the structure starts from where it is; where that branch ends, the stable solution nearest it is where it jumps to (the real Jacobian's determinant changes sign at each fold, so the unstable middle branch has it negative).

Distortion

One harmonic leaves out the rest: a nonlinear element turns a sine into a distorted wave. The Distortion tab steps the chain in time at one level and frequency (the exact FOH update, the nonlinear forces an input linear over each step, found by Newton) until a cycle repeats itself, and splits the steady response into harmonics. A symmetric nonlinearity (a cubic spring, a gap, friction) makes odd harmonics, 3X and 5X; an asymmetric one (a contact on one side) makes even ones too. Harmonics where the input was a pure sine are a second test of linearity.

Which force made this response?

Sometimes the force is what's wanted, and it can't be measured: a wheel on a rail, a gust, an impact inside a machine. The response can. With the force on one mass, sampled every h and linear between samples, the exact FOH update makes every sensor's sample a sum over the force's samples so far: y = G f, G lower triangular, its columns the impulse response shifted (the Markov parameters). Identifying the force is solving for f.

Why the plain inverse fails

G's singular values si fall over many decades: the chain filters out whatever is faster than its modes, and a displacement far from the force hardly sees its last samples. Written in G's singular directions, f = Σ(uiᵀy/si)vi: the noise's share of each direction is divided by si, so the smallest turn noise into a force thousands of times too large. The Picard plot (the Singular values tab) shows it: |uiᵀy| falls with si while the signal lasts, then levels off at the noise, and beyond that point dividing by si only amplifies noise.

Regularization

Truncated SVD keeps the first k directions and drops the rest. Tikhonov minimizes ‖Gf − y‖² + λ²‖f‖², which weights each direction by s²/(s² + λ²): kept where s ≫ λ, dropped where s ≪ λ. The L-curve plots the residual ‖Gf − y‖ against the force's size ‖f‖ as λ varies, on log axes: too small a λ and ‖f‖ explodes with the noise, too large and the residual grows as the force is smoothed away. Its corner balances them. With the noise's size known, the discrepancy principle picks the λ whose residual is that size. No λ brings back what G filtered out, though: a sensor that feels the force directly (an accelerometer on the force's own mass) helps far more than any choice of λ.

Things to try

Each of these loads a chain into the Simulation page and sets up the test.

  • A linear structure's FRF is the same at every level: measure at two or three and compare.
  • Swept up and down, a leaning peak jumps at different frequencies each way.
  • Hardening springs and gaps lean the peak up as the level rises; friction damps less.
  • Symmetric nonlinearities make odd harmonics from a pure sine.
  • y = Gf: G's tiny singular values turn noise into force, so the plain inverse is useless.
  • Tikhonov or truncated SVD keeps the directions the data can carry; the L-curve's corner picks λ.
  • A sensor that feels the force directly beats any regularization.
