A double pendulum

Two bobs on light rods, the second hung from the first. Its free-body diagram needs the rods' pulls: bob 2 across its rod gives one equation, but bob 1's needs rod 2's pull, found from bob 2 along its rod, and both bobs' accelerations have centripetal parts. Lagrange's equations need none of that. In the rods' angles

T = ½(m1 + m2)L1²θ̇1² + m2L1L2 cos(θ1 − θ2) θ̇1θ̇2 + ½m2L2²θ̇2²,   V = −(m1 + m2)gL1 cos θ1 − m2gL2 cos θ2,

and two differentiations give the whole nonlinear equations; the rods' pulls never appear, because the coordinates keep the rods' lengths and those forces do no work. For small swings M and K are T's and V's Hessians at rest. With equal bobs and rods, ω² = (2 ∓ √2)g/L: the rods swing together (θ2 = √2 θ1) or against each other (−√2 θ1).

Large swings are another matter. Released in a mode's shape at 40°, its period is 4 % longer and the shape doesn't hold. Released high, the motion is chaotic: two releases a millionth of a radian apart drift apart exponentially, doubling their difference about every half second, until nothing about one predicts the other. The Swing tab sets the whole equations against the linear ones, and the Chaos tab follows the twins.
