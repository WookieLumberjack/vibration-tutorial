A double pendulum

Two bobs m1 and m2 on light rods L1 and L2, the second hung from the first, each rod at its angle θ1, θ2 from the vertical.

Its free-body diagram needs the rods' pulls. Bob 2 across its rod gives one equation, since rod 2's pull has no part there (the figure's right). But bob 1's needs rod 2's pull, found from bob 2 along its rod, and both bobs' accelerations have centripetal parts. Lagrange's equations need none of that. In the rods' angles

T = \tfrac12 (m_1 + m_2) L_1^2 \dot\theta_1^2 + \tfrac12 m_2 L_2^2 \dot\theta_2^2 + m_2 L_1 L_2 \cos(\theta_1 - \theta_2)\,\dot\theta_1 \dot\theta_2 V = -(m_1 + m_2) g L_1 \cos\theta_1 - m_2 g L_2 \cos\theta_2

and two differentiations give the whole nonlinear equations; the rods' pulls never appear, because the coordinates keep the rods' lengths and those forces do no work. For small swings M and K are T's and V's Hessians at rest (cos → 1 in T, V to second order in the angles):

M = \begin{bmatrix} (m_1 + m_2) L_1^2 & m_2 L_1 L_2 \\ m_2 L_1 L_2 & m_2 L_2^2 \end{bmatrix} K = \begin{bmatrix} (m_1 + m_2) g L_1 & 0 \\ 0 & m_2 g L_2 \end{bmatrix}

Here M couples the angles and K doesn't. With equal bobs and rods, det(K − ω²M) = 0 is (ω²L/g)² − 4(ω²L/g) + 2 = 0, so

\omega^2 = (2 \mp \sqrt{2})\,\frac{g}{L}

and the rods swing together (θ2 = √2 θ1) or against each other (θ2 = −√2 θ1). Taking the elbow's bend φ = θ2 − θ1 as the second coordinate makes K couple too; the frequencies don't change.

Large swings are another matter. Released in a mode's shape at 40°, its period is about 4 % longer and the shape doesn't hold. Released high, the motion is chaotic shinbrot: two releases a millionth of a radian apart drift apart exponentially, doubling their difference about every half second, until nothing about one predicts the other. The Swing tab sets the whole equations against the linear ones, and the Chaos tab follows the twins.
