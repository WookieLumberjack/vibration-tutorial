The problem

A machine that runs with a little unbalance shakes with the force meeΩ². Bolted straight to the floor, all of it goes into the building; set on mounts, some of it is taken up by the machine's own motion. The same question arises the other way round: an instrument on a floor that shakes. Both are answered by one number, the transmissibility.

Transmissibility

The mount (k and c) pushes on the floor with kx + cẋ. For a machine of mass M on it, under F cos Ωt,

T = |Ffloor|/|F| = |k + iΩc| / |k − Ω²M + iΩc| = √(1 + (2ζr)²) / √((1 − r²)² + (2ζr)²),

with r = Ω/ωn. It is exactly the same as the motion that reaches a mass on a moving floor, X/Xg (the Simulation page's ground motion), so one curve serves for both. It is 1 at low speed (the mount passes the force on), peaks at resonance, and crosses 1 again at

r = √2,   whatever the damping.

Above √2 fn the mount isolates: the machine's inertia takes most of the force. Far above, T ≈ 1/r² (40 dB a decade). Below √2 fn it amplifies. So isolation needs a mount whose fn is well below the running speed: a soft one. The isolation is 1 − T, often quoted in dB, 20 log T.

Static deflection

A mount's stiffness under a machine of weight Mg is set by how far it sags, δ = Mg/k, and so

fn = (1/2π)√(g/δ) = 15.76/√δmm Hz.

The mass has gone: isolation is a matter of sag. 1 mm gives 15.8 Hz, 5 mm 7 Hz, 25 mm 3.2 Hz. For 90 % isolation (T = 0.1) undamped mounts need r² = 1 + 1/T = 11, fn = f/3.3; at 1500 rpm that is 7.5 Hz, 4.4 mm. The Design chart tab draws this for every speed. A very slow machine needs a very soft mount, which is why isolating low frequencies is hard.

The damping trade-off

Damping lowers the resonant peak, T ≈ 1/2ζ there, which matters while the machine runs up and down through it. But above √2 fn the damper passes force in proportion to speed, cΩX, and isolation gets worse: at high r, T ≈ 2ζ/r instead of 1/r². Mounts are a compromise: light damping, or damping that only works at resonance.

The relaxation isolator

Put the damper in series with a spring N k (an elastically supported damper). Near resonance the spring is stiff compared with the damper and the damper works; at high frequency the damper hardly moves, so the mount is just k and Nk side by side and T falls as 1/r² again, not 2ζ/r. Near resonance it passes a little more than a plain mount of the same ζ; it pays off well above it. Its dynamic stiffness is that of the Damping models page's Zener element.

Two stages

Mount the machine on an intermediate mass (a concrete block, a sub-frame), and mount that. There are two resonances, but above both each stage isolates on its own, and the transmissibility falls as 1/r⁴, 80 dB a decade. Ships' engines and sensitive instruments are mounted this way.

An inertia block

Bolting the machine to a heavy block and stiffening the mounts to keep the same sag leaves fn, and so T, unchanged. What it changes is the machine's own motion, mee/M well above resonance: a heavier M moves less. A block steadies the machine and lowers its centre of gravity; it doesn't isolate better.

Starting up

Running up to speed, the machine passes through the mounts' resonance. The unbalance's force there is smaller than at speed, (fn/f)² of it, and the faster the run up the less time the resonance has to build (the Single oscillator page's time constant): so lightly damped mounts are usually tolerable, and a slow run up or coast down through resonance is the hazard. The Start-up tab steps it exactly, the unbalance's force along the mount mee(Ω² cos φ + Ω' sin φ) as the speed climbs.

The machine here moves one way, up and down. On real mounts it also sways, rocks and twists: six modes, which the mounts couple unless they are placed (or tilted) about its centre of mass just so. That comes later, once the course has modes: see Rigid body on mounts.

Things to try

Each of these sets up the machine, the mounts and the plots (Things to try, top left).

  • Transmissibility is the same for force into the floor and motion from it; it falls below 1 only above √2 fn, whatever the damping.
  • A mount's fn is set by its static deflection alone: 15.76/√δmm Hz. Isolation needs a soft mount.
  • Damping lowers the resonant peak but worsens isolation above √2 fn; a relaxation mount gets the first without most of the second.
  • Two stages fall as 1/r⁴; an inertia block steadies the machine but doesn't change the transmissibility.
