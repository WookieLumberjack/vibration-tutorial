The shock response spectrum The chain's modes on a pulse

A design spectrum

The Shock spectrum page's idea, used for earthquakes: a single oscillator's peak response to the ground's motion, plotted against its period. An earthquake's spectrum is jagged and no two are alike, so a design code smooths and envelopes many into a design spectrum: a rise from the ground's own acceleration agS at T = 0, a plateau of 2.5 η agS from TB to TC, then falls as 1/T (constant velocity) and 1/T² (constant displacement). The page uses the shape of the European code's elastic spectrum. The ground type sets S and the corners: soft soil amplifies and widens the plateau. Damping scales it by η = √(10/(5 + ζ%)).

Participation and effective mass

A building on moving ground is pushed by −M1 ag (each floor's inertia, in coordinates relative to the ground), so each mode r is a single oscillator driven by Γr ag, with the participation factor

Γr = φrᵀM1 / φrᵀMφr.

Its peak modal coordinate is ΓrSa(Tr)/ωr², its floor displacements φr times that, and its base shear Meff,r Sa(Tr) with the effective modal mass Meff,r = (φrᵀM1)²/φrᵀMφr. The effective masses add up to the building's mass, so their running sum says how many modes are enough: codes ask for 90 %.

Combining the modes

The spectrum gives each mode's peak, not when it happens. Adding them (ABS) assumes they all peak together: safe, usually far too safe. SRSS, √Σ Rr², assumes they are independent, which holds when their frequencies are well apart. NRL (the largest plus the SRSS of the rest) sits between. When two modes are close they move together much of the time, and SRSS underestimates; the complete quadratic combination

R = √(ΣiΣj Ri ρij Rj)

keeps the cross terms, with Der Kiureghian's correlation ρij (1 on the diagonal, falling off as the frequencies part, faster the less the damping): ρ = 8ζ²(1 + r)r3/2 / ((1 − r²)² + 4ζ²r(1 + r)²) for equal damping and r = ωj/ωi. CQC is SRSS when the modes are far apart, so codes now use it throughout.

Checking against records

The page synthesizes seven ground motions whose spectra match the design spectrum (sines at random phases under a rise, strong-shaking and decay envelope, each scaled until its spectrum meets the target), steps the building on each mode by mode, exactly, and takes the mean of their peaks, as a code does with a set of records. The rules are estimates of that mean; each record scatters about it by some 10 %.
